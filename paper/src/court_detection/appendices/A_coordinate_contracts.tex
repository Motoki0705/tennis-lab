\section{Coordinate and Label Contracts}
\label{app:contracts}

\subsection{Transform direction and pose serialization}

Every stored rigid transform is a row-major \(4\times4\) homogeneous matrix
whose action on a column point is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}_b\\1\end{bmatrix}
    =\T{b}{a}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}_a\\1\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \T{a}{b}=\bigl(\T{b}{a}\bigr)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Rotations must be orthonormal with determinant \(+1\); uniform scale is carried
by the metric adapter rather than hidden inside a nominal rigid transform.
The camera transform in a generated sample is camera-to-scene.  World-to-camera
projection always uses its inverse.

The dataset stores raw camera/court transforms and intrinsics; the
augmentation-aware consumer derives, rather than duplicates, the pose target
schema
\texttt{pose10d\_camera\_to\_canonical\_row6d\_logf\_v1}.  Its scalar order is
\begin{center}
\texttt{tx, ty, tz, a11, a12, a13, a21, a22, a23, logf}.
\end{center}
Translation is the camera centre in canonical court metres, the six rotation
values are the first two rows of canonical-from-camera rotation, and focal is
in augmented model pixels.  Quaternion order is consequently irrelevant.

For camera centre \(\mathbf{c}^{\Court_i}\), identity is selected when
\(c_y<-10^{-6}\) m and the opposite mapping when \(c_y>10^{-6}\) m.  A camera
within \(10^{-6}\) m of the court mid-plane is rejected before rendering:
near/far is geometrically undefined there, and orientation is not used as a
silent tie-break.  Left/right always follows court-local \(X\).

\subsection{Visibility layers}

Four states remain distinct:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{in front}: positive camera depth;
  \item \emph{in frame}: the projected coordinate lies inside the image;
  \item \emph{renderer-supported}: a radius-one neighbourhood has sufficient
    alpha and positive rendered depth (not point-depth occlusion); and
  \item \emph{supervision eligible}: the generated conjunction used by the
    dense target.
\end{enumerate}
The reprojection loss uses the generated ground-truth supervision mask.  A
predicted score or predicted depth cannot alter it.  Augmentation recomputes
the image-frame geometry and supervision eligibility before collation; the padded batch
canvas is never treated as valid content.
