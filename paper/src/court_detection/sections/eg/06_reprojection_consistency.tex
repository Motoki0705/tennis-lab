\section{Differentiable Keypoint--Pose Consistency}
\label{sec:consistency}

Direct heatmap and pose losses supervise the same court through different
interfaces.  We make that shared meaning executable by requiring the dense
keypoints to agree with a differentiable projection of the pose prediction.

\subsection{Padding-aware continuous coordinates}

Batch collation pads every sample to the maximum height and width in the batch.
A spatial softmax over that canvas is incorrect: even a constant zero padding
logit can absorb probability mass when valid logits are negative.  Let
\(z_{bkyx}\) be the logit for batch item \(b\), physical keypoint channel \(k\),
and pixel \((x,y)\), and let \(V_b\) be the Boolean region belonging to the
sample's actual augmented image.  We compute
\begin{align}
  p_{bkyx}
    &=\frac{\mathbb{1}[(y,x)\in V_b]\exp(z_{bkyx}/\tau)}
      {\sum_{(y',x')\in V_b}\exp(z_{bky'x'}/\tau)}, \\
  \widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\mathrm{dense}}_{bk}
    &=\sum_{(y,x)\in V_b}p_{bkyx}[x,y]^{\top},
  \label{eq:masked_expectation}
\end{align}
where \(\tau>0\).  There is no sigmoid, top-\(k\), rounding, argmax, CPU
transfer, or detachment in this path.  The mask must be Boolean, share the
logit's device and spatial shape, and contain at least one valid pixel for every
sample; invalid inputs raise before reduction.

This expectation is restricted to the target-court singleton \KP contract.
Applying one expectation to a multi-court, multi-peak channel would produce a
coordinate between courts that has no physical meaning.

\subsection{Projection from the predicted camera}

Let \(\mathbf{X}_{bk}\) be the known canonical 3D coordinate in the exact
physical order stored by the pose target.  The decoded pose in
\cref{eq:pose10d,eq:rotation_decode} gives camera centre
\(\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_b\), canonical-from-camera rotation
\(\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_b\), and focal \(\widehat f_b\).  We transform and
project without using ground-truth translation, rotation, or focal length:
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\mathbf{X}}^{\Cam}_{bk}
    &=[\widehat X_{bk},\widehat Y_{bk},\widehat Z_{bk}]^{\top}
      =\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_b^{\top}
      (\mathbf{X}_{bk}-\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_b), \\
  \widehat{\Kmat}_b
    &=\begin{bmatrix}
       \widehat f_b&0&c_{x,b}\\
       0&\widehat f_b&c_{y,b}\\
       0&0&1
      \end{bmatrix}, \\
  \overline Z_{bk}
    &=\operatorname{sgn}_{+}(\widehat Z_{bk})
      \max(|\widehat Z_{bk}|,z_{\min}), \\
  \widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\mathrm{pose}}_{bk}
    &=\pi_{z_{\min}}(\widehat{\Kmat}_b,
       \widehat{\mathbf{X}}^{\Cam}_{bk})
      =\begin{bmatrix}
        \widehat f_b\widehat X_{bk}/\overline Z_{bk}+c_{x,b}\\
        \widehat f_b\widehat Y_{bk}/\overline Z_{bk}+c_{y,b}
       \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:predicted_projection}
\end{align}
Here \(\operatorname{sgn}_{+}(z)=1\) for \(z\geq0\) and \(-1\) otherwise;
the configured \(z_{\min}>0\) is both the safe-depth magnitude and cheirality
threshold.  The principal point \((c_{x,b},c_{y,b})\) comes from the
augmentation-aware target intrinsics because the pose head does not predict it.
It is geometry metadata, not leaked pose supervision.  The bounded coordinate
projection remains finite at invalid depth, while the cheirality term below
supplies its corrective gradient.

Before projection, the adapter verifies exact equality between
\texttt{semantic\_to\_physical} in the pose target and the singleton physical
index in every dense channel.  It also verifies that both branches use the same
augmented model-pixel frame.  No nearest-neighbour matching or implicit source-
to-model rescaling is permitted.

\subsection{Supervision-fixed robust loss}

Let \(m^{\mathrm{GT}}_{bk}\) indicate that a target-court point is eligible for
supervision according to generated in-front, in-frame, and renderer-support
authority.  Crucially, this mask is independent of the predicted keypoint score
and predicted depth.  With image diagonal
\(d_b=\sqrt{H_b^2+W_b^2}\), the normalized residual and coordinate loss are
\begin{align}
  r_{bk}
    &=\frac{\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\mathrm{dense}}_{bk}
              -\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\mathrm{pose}}_{bk}\|_2}{d_b}, \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coord}}
    &=\frac{\sum_{b,k}m^{\mathrm{GT}}_{bk}
      \operatorname{Huber}(r_{bk};\delta)}
      {\sum_{b,k}m^{\mathrm{GT}}_{bk}}.
  \label{eq:coordinate_loss}
\end{align}
We use the scalar Huber definition
\(
  \operatorname{Huber}(r;\delta)=\tfrac12r^2
\)
for \(r\leq\delta\), and
\(
  \delta(r-\tfrac12\delta)
\)
otherwise.  A sample with no eligible point contributes no coordinate residual,
but a batch with zero eligible points is a configuration/data error rather than
a silent zero loss.

Sending an eligible point behind the camera must not turn off supervision.  We add
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cheir}}=
  \frac{\sum_{b,k}m^{\mathrm{GT}}_{bk}
    \operatorname{softplus}\!\left(
      \frac{z_{\min}-\widehat Z_{bk}}{s_z}\right)}
       {\sum_{b,k}m^{\mathrm{GT}}_{bk}},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}}=
     \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coord}}+w_z\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cheir}},
  \label{eq:cheirality}
\end{equation}
with \(z_{\min},s_z>0\).  The full training objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}=
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dense}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pose}}
  +w_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}}.
  \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
After a configurable zero-weight warmup, \(w_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)\) increases
linearly to its declared value.  This prevents two untrained branches from
serving as each other's sole teacher while retaining direct supervision
throughout.  The standard mode propagates through both branches.  Two explicit
ablations stop the pose-side or dense-side gradient, respectively; they do not
change the forward residual.

\subsection{Constraining local-intersection shortcuts}
\label{sec:shortcut}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{figures/shortcut_mechanism}}
  \caption{\textbf{Constraining the local-intersection shortcut.}  A small
  neighbourhood around a white-line crossing is compatible with multiple
  physical identities and poses.  Rendered pose diversity changes the global
  arrangement while preserving such local evidence.  Direct pose supervision,
  global query attention, physical channel identity, and one shared projected
  \KP configuration penalize mutually inconsistent local outputs.  They do not
  make a local internal representation impossible; real-domain shortcut
  reduction remains an empirical question.}
  \label{fig:shortcut}
\end{figure}

Consider a crop containing two approximately perpendicular white lines.  Its
local appearance may be compatible with a service-line intersection, a
baseline/sideline corner, the far court instead of the near court, or a
different projective view.  A purely local classifier can exploit frequency
biases in the training set to choose among these identities.

\Method{} attacks this shortcut at three connected levels.
First, 3DGS rendering changes azimuth, range, height, field of view, target
court, partial coverage, and background context while retaining exact physical
identity and 6DoF.  The same local motif can therefore occur inside different
global configurations.  Second, the positionless court query can attend across
the spatial patch set and is directly trained to regress global pose; 2D RoPE
preserves where that evidence occurred.  Third,
\cref{eq:coordinate_loss} penalizes every supervision-eligible dense channel
that disagrees with the same projected rigid court.  Moving one eligible
junction independently increases the residual, while moving pose and dense
predictions together remains penalized by the direct losses in
\cref{eq:direct_losses}.

This mechanism does not ban local features---lines and junctions remain useful
image evidence---and it cannot determine which internal features the dense
branch uses.  It instead raises the loss whenever those outputs fail to support
a globally consistent, directly supervised camera--court explanation.  The
shortcut-focused diagnostic in \cref{sec:experiments} is therefore required to
test whether reliance actually decreases.  Exact pose labels make this
output-level constraint affordable: creating the same joint supervision by
hand would require calibrating every training image, including deliberately
off-centre and partially covered views.
