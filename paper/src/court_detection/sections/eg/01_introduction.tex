\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Court geometry is the reference frame for downstream tennis understanding.
Ball triangulation, player localization, camera fusion, and physically
meaningful trajectories all depend on knowing how image pixels map to a
regulation court.  In unconstrained video, however, the camera may move, crop
the court, view it obliquely, or include more than one court.  A useful court
detector must therefore recover a coherent global explanation rather than only
mark visible white-line fragments.

Most inexpensive annotations encourage exactly the opposite behavior.  A
heatmap label says where a junction lies, but not why that junction occupies
that pixel.  Two locally similar line intersections can correspond to different
physical corners, different target courts, or different camera poses.  A large
decoder may learn the dataset's local texture statistics while never acquiring
the projective structure required downstream.  Manual camera calibration can
remove this ambiguity, but attaching a metric 6DoF pose and intrinsics to every
training frame is substantially more expensive than clicking a sparse set of
image points.

Our central observation is that a reconstructed 3D Gaussian scene can act as a
\emph{geometric label generator}, not merely as a source of synthetic-looking
backgrounds.  Given one video, a public reconstruction system produces a 3DGS
scene, calibrated captured cameras, and a differentiable renderer
\citep{kerbl2023gaussian}.  We align the scene to a regulation court in metres,
then place new cameras along controlled three-dimensional paths.  Every render
inherits an exact camera matrix, a camera-to-court rigid transform, and the
projected geometry and renderer-supported pixel evidence of each canonical
court point.  The marginal cost of
annotating another view is consequently analytic projection rather than human
labeling.

This supervision becomes especially valuable when paired with an architecture
that is forced to use it.  \Method{} feeds \DINO{} patch tokens
\citep{simeoni2025dinov3} to a task Transformer containing a single learned
court query.  Rotary position encoding is applied to patch queries and keys,
but not to the court query itself.  The query is trained to aggregate the
complete patch set when predicting translation, rotation, and focal length,
while tapped patch
features feed deliberately lightweight \KP, line, and segmentation heads.  A
differentiable auxiliary objective then projects the known 3D court through
the predicted camera and matches those projections to continuous coordinates
extracted from the dense keypoint logits.  All supervision-eligible
correspondences share one pose, so mutually inconsistent junction outputs incur
an explicit penalty.

Our contributions are fourfold:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formulate a public-command 3DGS pipeline that turns a single accepted
  scene alignment into RGB, intrinsics, geometric validity, renderer support,
  physical \KP identity, and camera-to-canonical-court 6DoF supervision for
  arbitrarily many rendered views.

  \item We introduce a fail-closed metric alignment procedure with ground-plane
  ray intersection, a shared scale across two court hypotheses, rigid
  correspondence fitting, and disjoint fit/holdout evidence.  Its output is a
  typed scene--court contract rather than an unchecked homography.

  \item We construct deterministic circle/ellipse trajectories with planar or
  sinusoidal height, uniform 3D arc-length sampling, per-camera target-court
  resolution under the explicit v2 contract, and group-disjoint data splits.
  These paths expose the model to
  global view changes that are difficult to label manually.

  \item We couple a global pose-query encoder to lightweight dense heads through
  padding-aware, supervision-fixed keypoint--pose reprojection consistency and
  cheirality.  This design makes global projective agreement an explicit
  output-level training constraint rather than leaving it entirely emergent.
\end{enumerate}

We use \emph{annotation-light} and \emph{render-supervised} rather than
\emph{annotation-free}.  The scene alignment consumes measured court-line
evidence and must be validated.  The saving is that this cost is incurred once
per reconstructed scene; all accepted rendered frames receive their 6DoF and
dense labels automatically.
