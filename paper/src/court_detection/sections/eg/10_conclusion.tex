\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \Method, a court-detection system built around render supervision
from 3D Gaussian scenes.  Its fail-closed alignment recovers a metric
scene--court transform from held-out-validated line evidence; its trajectory
generator turns that transform into diverse calibrated views; and its labels
provide exact camera-to-court 6DoF, intrinsics, geometric validity,
renderer-supported pixel evidence, and physical keypoint identity without
per-render human annotation.  This data authority
enables a model that predicts one global camera hypothesis and couples it to
lightweight dense heads through differentiable reprojection and cheirality.

The central design lesson is that synthetic data is most useful here not because
it imitates more images, but because it exposes the latent geometry behind each
image at negligible marginal annotation cost.  By training local visual
evidence to participate in one globally consistent court explanation, the
system provides the coordinate foundation required for downstream 3D tennis
reconstruction.  Its empirical adoption remains governed by the preregistered
multi-seed and real-transfer protocol rather than by consistency loss alone.
