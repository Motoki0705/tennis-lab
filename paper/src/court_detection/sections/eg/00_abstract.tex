\begin{abstract}
Estimating a tennis court from a monocular image is often formulated as dense
keypoint or line detection.  That formulation is easy to supervise in two
dimensions, but it also admits a brittle shortcut: a detector can identify
locally plausible line intersections without recovering the single global
camera--court geometry that produced them.  Direct supervision of that geometry
would normally require expensive per-frame camera calibration.

We present \Method, a render-supervised court detector whose training data are
generated from metrically aligned 3D Gaussian scenes.  A fail-closed alignment
stage lifts image-line evidence onto an estimated ground plane, fits regulation
court templates with a shared metric scale, and validates the resulting rigid
transforms on held-out cameras.  Once this scene-level alignment is accepted,
controlled three-dimensional camera trajectories can be rendered with exact
intrinsics, camera-to-canonical-court 6DoF, in-front/in-frame state,
renderer-supported pixel evidence, and fourteen physically identified court
keypoints.  Thus the dominant annotation cost moves from
labeling every rendered image to validating one reusable scene alignment.

The detector combines a positionless global court query with patch-only 2D
rotary position encoding and lightweight dense decoders.  Its query regresses
camera translation, a continuous 6D rotation representation, and focal length.
A padding-aware differentiable keypoint expectation is constrained to agree
with the projection of the canonical court under that predicted geometry;
a fixed generated supervision mask and a cheirality penalty prevent the model
from choosing which residuals remain active.  The resulting objective permits
local line evidence but penalizes every supervision-eligible keypoint that
disagrees with one global camera hypothesis.  We provide the full coordinate
contracts, generation
algorithm, loss, failure conditions, and a preregistered evaluation protocol
without attributing unrecorded numerical gains to the method.
\end{abstract}
